# A Union Before Midnight — New Game Play Guide

This guide is for a **fresh India 1933 campaign** in the active local installation, `AUBM Terrain Prototype P1`. It reflects the installed 27-BALANCE1, 27-ROSTER1 and ART2 artwork deltas.

## Before you begin

Completely close Darkest Hour, reopen it, select **AUBM Terrain Prototype P1**, and start the 1933 India scenario. The menu should say **PLAY 27-ROSTER1** and the scenario title should include **27-ROSTER1**. ART2 is an artwork-only follow-up, so it deliberately does not replace that gameplay label; its install receipt is `AUBM_ART2_INSTALL_RECEIPT.json` in the mod folder.

Use a new campaign. The new balance, roster and individual portraits are not a conversion for an old save. In particular, saved leader records keep their old portrait references.

## The first week: what is supposed to happen

The opening is deliberately short.

1. **Opening premise:** read and acknowledge the initial situation.
2. **First 72 hours:** choose the opening cabinet. These are genuine emphases, not one mandatory answer.
3. **6 January:** choose the method of union. This establishes your political starting character and unlocks the normal Indian programme.

The old mass of route/cabinet pop-ups has been retired for fresh games. You should not be forced into a War Cabinet, a major alliance, or a Japanese decision in the first days. If a decision says it is unavailable, hover it first: most locks are date, war, union, prerequisite-programme or mutually-exclusive commitment locks rather than a hidden random failure.

## Your first two years
the opening constraint is **supply and raw materials**, not merely IC.
India is built to become a great power, but 

- Pick an industrial package for the campaign you intend to play. The larger factory option is no longer automatically best: the middle and lower options often give more energy, metal, oil, roads, stores, research or manpower relief.
- Treat the resource chain as part of every large factory, armour or fleet plan. Added resources are permanent provincial production, not an immediate national stockpile refill. Trade, convoys, infrastructure and oil conservation still matter.
- Keep a supply reserve before starting a large modernisation run. India can outbuild its supply system if it builds factories, armour and ships simultaneously.
- Do not expect every early political event to be a dissent optimisation puzzle. The rebuilt choices are intended to trade manpower, supply, security, research, transport and political stability.

### A useful opening template

For a long neutral or anti-Japan campaign: take a balanced industry/resource option, protect supply production, build a few escorts/transports and interceptors first, and use the early years for infrastructure, doctrine and specialist units. You can begin a serious carrier/surface programme later without abandoning naval catch-up.

For an early continental campaign: favour manpower, supplies, roads, mountain/jungle doctrine and a practical air force before expensive capital ships. Your naval programme remains viable; it simply does not need to consume every spare resource in 1933.

## Military expectations

### Army

India begins with a large inherited force but not a fully modern field army. Provincial Forces releases the inherited locked state divisions; if you have not taken it, the same safety release occurs when India first enters war. Use the early army events to decide whether you want mobile formations, frontier/mountain forces, a large territorial army, or balanced training.

Mountain and jungle forces are worthwhile in the theatres India is most likely to fight: Burma, Malaya, Indochina, Tibet, Central Asia and the Caucasus. Engineers help in the same terrain and are no longer a cosmetic choice.

### Air force

The **Airfield Security Act** is meant to be a real force-development decision: it provides early aircraft, guards and base improvements. Choose the branch that matches your plan—interception, a forward interceptor/bomber group, or air transport—not simply the highest immediate division number. The later air-group decision uses normal production models and contracts; it is not a free instant air wing.

### Navy

India has a deliberately strong catch-up yard programme. The mature standard is **25% faster construction in peace** and **40% in war**, before ordinary technology and other modifiers. That is powerful, but it is not a free navy: oil, metal, rares, escorts, bases, convoys, naval air and commanders remain the limiting factors.

Build the navy in layers. A useful order is convoy/escort protection and transports, then naval-air cover and screens, then carriers or surface ships. A large fleet without fuel, screens or trained leaders will still lose to an established British, American or Japanese navy.

## People and research

The opening roster gives you real specialisation rather than interchangeable copies.

- **Cabinet:** select a department head for the campaign you are actually running. Katari favours carrier/naval-air coordination; Soman heavy surface combat; Choudri escorts and transports; Ahsan submarines. Thimayya favours mobile forces, Shrinagesh frontier troops, Mukerjee air defence and Aspy Engineer maritime aviation.
- **Land research:** compare Cariappa, Thimayya, Quetta-Delhi, Messervy, the armoured school and Cochin rather than always using Quetta.
- **Naval research:** surface, escort/submarine, carrier, fleet doctrine, carrier doctrine and undersea doctrine all have a purposeful Indian team.
- **Air research:** fighters, transport/bomber airframes, tactical support, naval aviation and long-range doctrine have different best fits.

The commander reserve is large enough for a late great-power military. It adds fictional `(R)` officers over time, but does not give automatic rank, skill or combat bonuses. ART2 gives each reserve officer an individual portrait in a new campaign; two historically sourced portraits remain private-use artwork and are excluded from any public pack.

## Diplomacy: choose a campaign, not a menu loop

India can remain sovereign, cooperate with a great power through a separate command relationship, or formally join an alliance. A formal alliance shares its wars; a separate-command agreement preserves more control but carries fewer automatic protections. While a major commitment is active it is exclusive. At peace, withdrawal/realignment is intentionally possible later; switching between every bloc during one war is not.

| Direction | What it feels like | Main caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Allied | The safest economic and naval starting point. | Formal entry can inherit every Allied war. |
| German | A harder resource and continental-war campaign. | Germany can collapse early; do not assume Berlin will carry the war. |
| Soviet | A continental and Central Asian campaign. | A deep Soviet outcome requires holding real strategic centres, not a quick border dash. |
| Japanese | The most bespoke Southeast Asian campaign. | The Delhi–Tokyo compact is more controllable than immediately inheriting all Japanese wars. |
| Sovereign | Maximum freedom and the best fit for choosing your own wars. | You must build your own trade, diplomacy, timing and war justification. |

The game should not force you to decide on Japan early. Tokyo's early proposition has a real free defer and is suppressed if you are already fighting Japan or bound by another major commitment.

### Japan compact and an independent Soviet war

The **Delhi-Tokyo strategic compact** is not an engine alliance. From 1940,
India may use **Open an Independent Northern War against the Soviet Union**
while the compact is active, provided India has not formally allied with Japan,
is not already in a multi-country alliance war, and has 700 money and 1,800
supplies. The declaration is India's alone; Tokyo then responds with fixed
odds: 55% preserves Soviet neutrality and sends 500 supplies, 25% applies Far
East pressure without war, 15% joins the Soviet war, and 5% condemns Delhi.
The separate-war docket is an additional response path when a partner crisis
exists; Japan does **not** need to be at war with the USSR for India to open
this campaign.

## Recommended first playthrough: wait, then liberate Asia

If you want the "India waits until Japan is the aggressor" campaign:

1. Stay sovereign through the early political and industrial programme.
2. From 1940, enable the sovereign Liberator programme.
3. Remain at peace with Japan. The programme watches countries that were peaceful with Japan when observation began.
4. If Japan newly attacks one of them, you receive a notification. You may keep waiting without cost.
5. When prepared, open **Respond to Japanese Aggression**, review it, then explicitly confirm war. The decision costs **+2 dissent** and does not automatically put India in the British or American alliance.

This gives you a clean anti-Japan war without having to be an Allied client. It also permits an independent Soviet war if the normal diplomatic/war conditions allow it; one war does not silently cancel the other.

Important limitation: the observer recognises a new Japanese attack after its peaceful baseline. It does not relabel an old war already in progress when you enable it.

## What winning a long war looks like

Do not expect one captured province to award an empire. Major outcomes require control and a sustained hold.

- **Southeast Asia:** liberation of Japanese-occupied friendly territory and sea-lane work can build a regional result without stealing land from the friendly owner. The full theatre needs a mix of land categories and sea lanes.
- **Indochina:** if Japan's puppet U03 exists, capture Hanoi, Dong Hoi, Da Nang, Saigon and Can Tho and hold them for 30 days. A paid negotiation can create either an independent partner or an Indian puppet. It does not automatically transfer Japanese provinces to India.
- **China:** the higher outcomes distinguish an independent China from an Indian-puppet outcome. Read the warning before accepting an independence offer: it may close the stronger puppet demand.
- **Soviet war:** a northern provisional recognition threshold is deliberately deeper. Hold Baku/Astrakhan/Stalingrad **or** Samarkand/Tashkent/Alma-Ata/Sverdlovsk for 60 days while at war. The later Soviet campaign has still deeper 90-day requirements. A limited peace is not the same as creating Central Asian republics.
- **Japan:** a limited southern settlement is weaker than a decisive victory. The optional home-island track distinguishes a full armistice from limited peace.

Use the **LIBERATOR3 - Campaign Progress Board** to read relevant conditions. Its status entries are informational; they should not spend time, money or a game day. It is the safe way to check a hold or cooldown rather than guessing.

## The world now responds to India

Britain, Japan, the USSR, Germany, Italy, the USA and Australia have guarded response priorities when actually fighting human India. The aim is not to create free enemy armies; it is to make theatres, ports, garrisons and counterattacks matter more.

Expect Germany to need help in Africa or Finland, Italy to retain more overseas defence, Britain to be less inclined toward speculative invasions, and opponents to pay more attention to an Indian front. This is still a strategy-game AI, not a promise of a particular historical timeline. A well-timed Indian second front can remain very powerful.

## What to test and report

This is a new-campaign playtest build. The structural checks passed, but no one should claim a completed native balance playtest yet. The most valuable notes are:

- the exact event title and option you chose;
- date, route and whether India was at peace or war;
- resources, supplies, dissent and manpower before and after;
- whether an unavailable decision explained its lock clearly;
- a screenshot of any unreadable event text, portrait, tooltip or wrong asset;
- a save immediately before a crash, unwanted peace, alliance switch or settlement.

Do not continue an older save just to test this balance/roster/art build. Keep it as a record of the prior campaign; start a fresh India 1933 game for a clean result.

## Short version

Build resources with industry, do not rush every expensive programme at once, pick specialists for the war you intend to fight, stay sovereign until you actually want a commitment, and use the progress board before accepting a long-war settlement. India is designed to become formidable—not to begin as a finished empire.
