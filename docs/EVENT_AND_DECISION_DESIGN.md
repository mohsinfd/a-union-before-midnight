# Event and Decision Design

## Player-Facing Rule

A **decision** is an authorization the player may defer. Its prerequisites and
costs must be visible before selection, and its window normally remains open
through 1964.

Priced decisions use a three-part visibility contract: the decision remains
listed when its strategic prerequisites are met, `decision_desc` lists every
programme and reserve threshold, and the decision becomes selectable when at
least one programme is affordable. Each action has its own reserve trigger, so
Darkest Hour may hide an unaffordable action without hiding the knowledge that
the programme exists.

An **event** is a consequence that cannot be postponed: a constitutional
deadline, foreign crisis, implementation dispute, foreign reply, battlefield
milestone or peace settlement.

Automatic one-option Indian events may deliver consequences or rewards, but
cannot deduct money or supplies. A mandatory procedure can change doctrine,
organization, dissent or flags; an expensive programme must be a decision or
offer a genuinely different low-cost response.

## Current Audit

- Custom entries: 4,343 across 56 isolated modules: 3,706 Indian entries and
  637 foreign replies.
- Decisions: 103.
- Automatic events, deadlines, crises, objectives, negotiations and foreign
  replies: 4,240.
- Required persistent systems include the annual Union Budget, strategy and
  commitment reviews, the War Cabinet, country campaign ledgers, wartime
  accounts, mobilisation, occupation upkeep and postwar settlement.
- Coalition entry: available during peace or war, unless India is fighting the
  prospective partner.
- Strategic path expiry: 1964.

The production validator enforces the classification rule for single-action
Indian events and rejects decision windows that silently expire before the
campaign ends.

## Force Reward Contract

An event-funded production order represents contracts, tooling or an inherited
hull already in progress. It uses the current researched model at normal daily
IC cost and supplies remaining days for the first item. Later items in the same
serial line begin at zero progress, so extending a line never duplicates the
event award. Fixed cheap IC costs and zero-progress pseudo-rewards are invalid.

A direct formation reward appears immediately inside a named corps, uses the
latest researched model and is field-ready. The event deducts its manpower
explicitly, so it cannot disguise a future reinforcement bill. Production
orders remain the preferred contract for capital ships and other formations
whose construction time is part of the strategic choice.

## Choice Quality

Actions should trade different currencies rather than repeat one reward at
three prices. Useful tensions include:

- money now versus permanent revenue;
- organization versus morale or repair capacity;
- central capacity versus provincial legitimacy;
- alliance matériel versus sovereign command;
- maximum war effort versus postwar leverage;
- decolonization versus relations with existing empires;
- battlefield readiness versus dissent and manpower.

Labels state the important immediate costs and rewards. Descriptions explain
the political problem, not hidden mechanics or instructions.

## Historical Premise

The divergence grows from the Round Table Conferences, the Poona settlement,
the proposed all-India federation and the British constitutional White Paper.
The Lahore Accord and a sovereign 1933 union are alternate history; its
institutions, leaders and strategic pressures are grounded in period actors and
plausible state interests.
