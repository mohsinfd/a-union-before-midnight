# Wartime Campaign Map

This document is the canonical gameplay map for A Union Before Midnight Alpha
18. Event code may add flavour, but it must preserve these rules.

## Common Lifecycle

1. The War Cabinet records one current command relationship: Allied, German,
   Soviet, Japanese or sovereign.
2. India may join a formal coalition, retain a separate-command compact, switch
   coalitions through the transfer rule, or declare a country-specific war.
3. A campaign brief publishes the Indian-controlled objective before rewards
   are possible.
4. Capturing the objective opens a live settlement claim. Losing it suspends
   the claim; recovering it restores the same file.
5. A surviving opponent receives a disclosed accept, counter or refusal roll.
   The foreign event never executes peace. Delhi ratifies only the selected
   pairwise armistice.
6. Annexation never silently becomes a final map. India must restore a
   sovereign government, create a protected state, or pay the dissent,
   belligerence and annual upkeep of direct rule.
7. A refusal returns after a guarded cooldown. A later genuine war can open a
   new lifecycle without duplicating the previous reward. If a government is
   annexed by somebody else while its answer is in transit, the dead response
   lock is released and its file can reopen if the state later returns.
8. India can change route without losing verified campaign credit. A dead or
   hostile partner returns India to sovereign command; the current valid route
   determines the eventual Delhi congress.

## Entry And Revision

- **Allied formal coalition:** from 1937, the Coalition Docket opens a separate
  choice between London and Washington. India cannot join while fighting either
  Allied leader. A separate Allied treaty conference becomes available in 1938.
- **German formal coalition:** from 1937, India may enter Berlin's coalition if
  Germany exists and India is not fighting it. The deeper separate-command
  Berlin compact follows the European-Soviet crisis from 1940.
- **Soviet formal coalition:** from 1937, India may enter Moscow's coalition if
  the Soviet Union exists and India is not fighting it. The socialist
  conference also offers equal, supervised and autonomous Indian terms.
- **Japanese formal coalition or compact:** from 1937, India may join Tokyo's
  wars formally or retain a bilateral separate-command compact. Under the
  compact, an Indian-Soviet war does not automatically become Japan's war.
- **Sovereign command:** available whenever India leaves a coalition or rejects
  permanent alignment. Autonomous Indian socialism is sovereign command with a
  socialist charter, not membership in Moscow's alliance.
- **Revision:** the War Cabinet remains permanent. India may attack a former
  partner, change coalition, or prosecute any bilateral campaign; cleanup and
  partner-collapse events normalize the route without deleting verified
  campaign credit.

## Route Contract

| Indian command | Coalition choice | Independent war option | Failure recovery | Peace payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Allied | Britain or United States | Separate-command cooperation or any bilateral war | London/Washington failover, then sovereign fallback | Allied Delhi congress |
| German | Berlin coalition or cobelligerent compact | Parallel Indian war against Britain, the Soviet Union or any state | German collapse or rupture returns sovereign command | Continental Delhi congress |
| Soviet | Moscow coalition, equal compact or supervised compact | Autonomous Indian socialism and any bilateral war | Moscow refusal, collapse or direct Indian-Soviet war returns sovereign command | Republican Delhi congress |
| Japanese | Tokyo coalition or Delhi-Tokyo separate command | Independent Soviet war, southern sphere or any bilateral war | Rejection, rupture or Japanese collapse returns sovereign command | Asian Delhi congress |
| Sovereign | No permanent coalition | Every modeled country | Reversal and recovery remain country-specific | Sovereign Delhi congress |

Every route uses the same visible contract: a brief names the capital or
theatre objective; capture opens a settlement; loss suspends it; recovery
restores it; a surviving government answers; and annexation opens a separate
constitutional choice. A distant victory outside the four named route doctrines
still counts toward the current route's Delhi congress.

## Allied Route

- **Eastern Ocean Command:** defeat Japan or secure the southern resource arc.
- **Continental Expeditionary Command:** earn an Indian-controlled result in
  Germany, Italy, France or Turkey.
- **Anti-Colonial Liberation Mandate:** turn western, African or Gulf victories
  into sovereign settlement standing.
- **Sovereign Free Command:** earn an independent decisive victory while
  cooperating with the Allies.
- **Postwar:** choose a sovereign concert, Indian security sphere or renewed
  strategic autonomy.

## German Route

- **Eurasian Link:** open an Indian front against the Soviet southern system.
- **Imperial Dismantlement:** attack Britain and its Indian Ocean structure.
- **Southern Resource Race:** establish Indian, rather than Japanese or German,
  primacy in Southeast Asia.
- **Sovereign Parallel War:** coordinate with Berlin without surrendering
  India's campaign or settlement authority.
- **Postwar:** choose continental balance, an Indian security sphere or an end
  to military alignment while preserving technical ties.

## Soviet Route

- **Anti-Fascist Expedition:** fight Germany and its European partners under an
  independent Indian command ledger.
- **Anti-Imperial Ocean War:** attack Britain and colonial systems without
  transferring the result to Moscow.
- **Republican Asian Order:** intervene in China, Siam or the Japanese war for
  an Asian republican settlement.
- **Autonomous Indian Socialism:** cooperate ideologically while measuring
  victory only through an Indian campaign.
- **Postwar:** choose sovereign republics, a protected security belt or
  autonomous Indian socialism outside a permanent bloc.

## Japanese Route

- **Indian Southern Sphere:** India leads in Burma, Malaya, the East Indies and
  Australia; Japan retains China, the Philippines and Pacific primacy.
- **Independent Soviet Campaign:** India may fight the Soviet Union while the
  separate-command Delhi-Tokyo compact keeps Japan outside that war.
- **Indian Ocean First:** India campaigns through the Gulf, Suez and East
  Africa while Japan concentrates eastward.
- **Equal Asian Command:** a decisive or two-theatre Indian result establishes
  Delhi as Tokyo's strategic equal.
- **Postwar:** choose an equal Asian concert, Indian ocean sphere or strategic
  separation after victory.

## Sovereign Route

- **Indian Ocean League:** combine southern and western settlements into a
  network of ports, access treaties and sovereign partners.
- **Continental Security Arc:** campaign through Afghanistan, Tibet, Xinjiang,
  Central Asia and the Soviet south.
- **World Balancer:** compel any great power to negotiate directly with Delhi.
- **Republican Federation:** measure success through restored governments and
  guarantees instead of automatic annexation.
- **Postwar:** choose an open league, defended sphere or self-contained
  great-power republic.

## Bespoke Opponents

- **Great powers:** Britain, Germany, Soviet Union, Japan and United States have
  limited and decisive objectives, reversals, recoveries, armistices and
  post-annex settlements.
- **Regional powers:** Persia, Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Yemen, Afghanistan,
  Tibet, Xinjiang, both Chinas, Siam, Italy, France, Turkey, East Indies,
  Netherlands, Australia, Portugal, New Zealand, Ethiopia and South Africa use
  capital-based country files and provisional constitutional settlements.
- **Southern Japan partnership:** Malaya, Indonesia and Australia retain a
  dedicated division-of-labour settlement with explicit foreign responses.
- **Regional armistice files:** Persia, Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Yemen, Oman,
  Afghanistan, Tibet and Xinjiang answer Delhi independently on disclosed
  60/25/15 terms; a refusal creates only a country-specific retry cooldown.

## Every Other Sovereign State

The generated global matrix covers 210 country tags in Europe, Asia, the
Americas and Africa, including states created later by loaded events. Together
with five bespoke great powers and 21 bespoke regional opponents, this gives
India 236 practical country-specific campaigns. Each uses the same declaration,
brief, capital objective, reversal, recovery, response, pairwise peace, refusal
retry and post-annex settlement contract. These campaigns remain available
regardless of India's strategic route. Declarations, acceptances, counteroffers,
refusals and vanished-government audits use country-specific callbacks; no
single monolithic event loops over every opponent or settlement command.

## Economy And Political Cost

- Mobilisation supplies manpower but charges supplies and dissent; a second
  service call is available only after the trained pool falls below 150.
- War finance grants no passive infinite treasury. Bonds and external credit
  advance cumulative debt; taxation costs more dissent; ordinary revenue pays
  no lump sum.
- Direct occupation advances one annual upkeep tier per retained settlement.
  Four tiers and an overextension premium prevent a cost-free continental map;
  devolving a mandate can step the recurring burden back down.
- A route achievement grants standing and a limited material reward. Territory
  and government status always remain separate settlement decisions.
