# COMMAND I — February 1941 Operational Plan

This plan is written for `COMMAND1_India_1941_February_1.eug`, dated 1 February 1941. It is not a generic India guide. Formation locations, force counts, enemy estimates, terrain, infrastructure, air ranges and sea routes come from that campaign and the installed Darkest Hour map.

## 1. Commander's intent

India will enter the Axis war only after preparing two mutually supporting theatres:

1. **Eastern theatre:** seize Singapore and Malaya quickly, creating the forward naval and air base needed for later operations against Japan, Indonesia, Australia or the Philippines.
2. **Western theatre:** enter through Basra by sea, destroy the exposed British corridor through Iraq and Palestine, seize Suez, and—when the German-Soviet war begins—drive through Persia to Baku and then Astrakhan.

The first 90 days are not a race to occupy the most map area. The decisive objectives are **Singapore, Suez and Baku**. Astrakhan is an exploitation objective. Australia, Indonesia, central Siberia and deep Africa are explicitly outside the first campaign phase.

## 2. What is exact and what is estimated

- Province connections, route distances, terrain, infrastructure, formation locations and known enemy formations are taken from game data or the save.
- Transit times are planning estimates. Darkest Hour recalculates arrival using unit speed, infrastructure, weather, effective supply efficiency, combat delay and air/naval interference. Treat the stated dates as decision windows, not promises.
- Fuel and supply figures are the saved formations' nominal daily consumption. Actual consumption changes with movement, combat, mission intensity, reinforcements and the convoy system.
- Enemy reinforcement estimates are intelligence assessments based on forces visible in the save and their distance from the theatre.

## 3. Strategic readiness gate

Do not enter the war before all five conditions are met:

| Condition | Minimum | Preferred |
|---|---:|---:|
| Supplies in stockpile | 25,000 | 30,000 |
| Dissent | below 1% | zero |
| Western staging | motor and Caucasus assault groups in Karachi | all first-lift units in Karachi |
| Fleet | split into commanded, screened task forces | rehearsed at Karachi/Rangoon |
| Airbase reserve | 15 deployable levels | 20 deployable levels |

The current stockpile is about **11,117 supplies** and **128,480 oil**. The whole force's saved nominal consumption is approximately **293 supplies and 586 oil per day** at full activity. With no new production, that is only 38 days of supplies but 219 days of oil. The immediate strategic shortage is supplies, not fuel.

The formal Axis route is operationally preferable because Iraqi access turns the western deployment from a 100-day land march into a 4–6 day sea lift. If Germany attacks the USSR before India is ready, remain neutral for two or three weeks rather than launching an unprepared campaign. If no German-Soviet war exists by 1 August 1941, fight Britain first and postpone the Soviet operation.

## 4. Order of battle and assignments

### Army Group East — Singapore and Malaya

**Assault Wave One — exactly five divisions**

- Coromandel Marine Division: 3 marine divisions
- Andaman Marine Division: 2 marine divisions

Five is the saved amphibious stacking limit. Do not add a sixth division to the initial assault.

**Assault Wave Two — nine divisions**

- I Southern Corps: 3 marines
- III Eastern Corps: 3 marines
- Eastern Long-Range Group: 3 mountain divisions

**Theatre reserve**

- Ceylon Territorial Command: 2 motorized and 1 light-armour division
- Eastern Army Command at Akyab: 5 infantry and 1 headquarters

Wave One takes the port. Wave Two expands the bridgehead. The territorial formation supplies mobile pursuit. The army command follows only after Singapore is secure; it should not congest the beachhead during the assault.

### Army Group West — Iraq, Palestine and Suez

**Mobile column — 18 divisions**

- Four motor corps: 12 motorized divisions
- Northern Army Command: 5 infantry and 1 headquarters

Strategically redeploy these formations from Muzaffarabad to Karachi before war. Their saved consumption is approximately **23.3 supplies and 89.7 fuel per day** before attaching other forces.

### Army Group Caucasus — Persia, Baku and Astrakhan

**Main mountain column — 21 divisions**

- 4th, 5th, 6th and 7th Mountain Corps: 12 mountain divisions
- Pioneer Group: 3 divisions
- Western Army Command: 5 infantry and 1 headquarters

**Eastern Persian fixing column — 12 divisions**

- 3rd Delhi Mountain Corps
- 8th Baluchistan Mountain Corps
- 1st and 2nd Mountain Corps

The main column travels by sea to Basra and attacks through Ahvaz. The fixing column remains near Gwadar and advances only to pin Persian forces or exploit an empty route. It is not allowed to dictate the campaign timetable.

**Operational reserve — 12 divisions**

- Four armoured corps at Surat: each contains 1 armour and 2 motorized divisions

These saved armoured divisions are model 0 and the motorized divisions are model 1. They are not the force to open mountain passes. Upgrade them and commit them only after Tabriz or Baku opens a suitable exploitation corridor. The four corps consume about **19.1 supplies and 106.1 fuel per day** when active.

## 5. Naval organization

The 34-ship Karachi fleet is too large, has no commander, and is slightly short of screens. Divide it before war.

### Carrier Striking Force

- 2 CV-III from the main fleet
- the CV-III currently in the Indian Ocean group
- approximately 4 modern light cruisers and 4 modern destroyers
- commander: Bhaskar Soman

Keep this force fast. Do not attach battleships or slow transports.

### Amphibious Covering Force

- CV-II *Vikrant* and *Viraat*
- light carriers as available
- enough light cruisers and destroyers to maintain at least one screen per capital ship
- commander: Ram Dass Katari

### Battle and Bombardment Force

- the modern battleships, heavy cruisers and remaining screens
- divide again if necessary to preserve capital-to-screen balance

Do not risk a 34-ship uncommanded mass in one battle. Keep the regional Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal battleship groups available for local bombardment or convoy cover.

There are nine troop-transport groups. Each has three 1936 transports, two destroyers and one light carrier, giving a total lift capacity of 27 divisions. Stage Groups 1–6 at Karachi and Groups 7–9 at Rangoon. The western lift can therefore move 18 divisions at once; the eastern lift can move nine.

## 6. Air plan and forward bases

Current practical mission radii are approximately:

- naval bomber: 1,250 km
- tactical bomber: 900 km
- close air support: 600 km

Rangoon to Singapore is about 1,920 km and Port Blair to Singapore about 1,683 km. Neither base can provide normal land-based air cover over Singapore. The first assault therefore depends on carrier aviation and shore bombardment.

Recommended deployment of 20 saved airbase levels:

| Site | Levels | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Tavoy | 2 | Bay of Bengal relay and Malaya approach |
| Singapore | 4 | deploy after capture; main eastern hub |
| Basra | 4 | initial western air bridge |
| As Zarqa or Jerusalem | 2 | canal campaign support |
| Tabriz | 3 | Baku attack support |
| Baku | 3 | Caspian and Astrakhan operations |
| Astrakhan | 2 | Volga consolidation |

Port Blair already has airbase 7, Rangoon 4, Colombo 10 and Karachi 10. Adding more there is a lower priority than creating forward steps.

Initial air movements:

- Transfer one four-wing interceptor group and four naval bombers to Karachi for western fleet defence.
- Retain carrier cover for Singapore until the port falls.
- Immediately after Singapore falls, rebase four interceptors and four naval or tactical bombers there.
- Move tactical and close-support groups to Basra only after an airbase is deployed and supplies are accumulating.

## 7. Route and march tables

### Eastern route: Rangoon to Singapore by sea

`Rangoon → Gulf of Martaban → East Andaman Sea → South Malacca Strait → Coast of Malaysia → Singapore`

- route distance: about 2,095 km
- planned transport time: 4–6 days
- assault and unloading allowance: 1–3 additional days
- current objective defence: one British Singapore Fortress division
- Singapore facilities: naval base 10, airbase 8, anti-air 6, no land fort in the save

The land route through Siam is both politically unavailable and operationally inferior:

`Rangoon → Pegu → Moulmein → Tavoy → Prachuap Khiri Khan → Surat Thani → Alor Setar → Ipoh → Kuala Lumpur → Johor → Singapore`

It is roughly 2,305 km and about 69 movement days before combat for ordinary foot formations under the current terrain and infrastructure assumptions.

After Singapore falls, advance on two axes:

- western axis: `Singapore → Johor → Kuala Lumpur → Ipoh → Alor Setar`
- eastern axis: `Johor → Kuantan → Kota Bahru`, then turn west toward Ipoh

Important legs include Johor–Kuala Lumpur, about 296 km through mountain terrain, and Johor–Kuantan, about 267 km. The visible Malayan provinces north of Singapore are empty in the save, but assume Commonwealth reinforcements begin arriving within two to four weeks.

### Western insertion: Karachi to Basra

`Karachi → Mouths of the Indus → Gulf of Oman → Persian Gulf → Basra`

- route distance: about 2,388 km
- transport time: 4–6 days
- Basra facilities: naval base 4, infrastructure 40, no airbase

Unload no more than the first 18 divisions until the local supply depot is functioning. The sea route is the operational hinge of the plan.

### Rejected main route: Gwadar to Tehran

`Gwadar → Zahedan → Kerman → Esfahan → Tehran`

- distance: about 1,794 km
- baseline march time: about 112 days before combat
- Gwadar–Zahedan alone: about 503 km through mountains and infrastructure 10, approximately 42 baseline days

Even the better weighted route through Birjand and Mashhad is approximately 103 days. Use it only as a secondary fixing advance.

### Basra to Baku

`Basra → Ahvaz → Hamadan → Tabriz → Baku`

| Leg | Distance | Terrain/infrastructure | Baseline foot time |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Basra–Ahvaz | 123 km | marsh, infra 30 | about 5 days |
| Ahvaz–Hamadan | 388 km | mountain, infra 30 | about 11 days |
| Hamadan–Tabriz | 415 km | mountain, infra 30 | about 12 days |
| Tabriz–Baku | 402 km | hills, infra 60 | about 6 days |

Total: about 1,328 km and 33 baseline movement days. Plan on **45–60 days** after combat, reorganization and supply friction.

Visible opposition is 3 Persian divisions at Ahvaz, 3 at Busehr and 6 at Tehran. Baku presently has 5 Soviet divisions. Allow for 8–12 defenders by the time India arrives, with 15 as the adverse case.

### Baku to Astrakhan

`Baku → Makhachkala → Khasavyurt → Elista → Astrakhan`

- distance: about 1,109 km
- baseline foot movement: about 45 days
- mobile planning estimate: 20–30 days before major combat delay
- present Astrakhan defence: 4 Soviet divisions
- nearby visible reserves: 2 at Stalingrad, 2 at Rostov, 1 at Sukhumi

The Elista leg crosses poor infrastructure and is the logistical break point. Do not launch it until Baku holds a functioning depot and the armoured group has fuel.

### Basra to Suez

`Basra → An Najaf → Karbala → As Zarqa → Amman → Al Kerak → Jerusalem → Tel Aviv → El Arish → Al Qantarah → Port Said/Suez`

- route distance: approximately 2,100 km
- foot baseline: about 89 days
- mobile planning estimate: 20–30 days before combat delays
- first leg: Basra–An Najaf, about 390 km through damaged desert infrastructure

Visible opposition includes 1 Canadian division at An Najaf, 1 British division at As Zarqa, 1 at El Arish, 3 at Port Said and 4 at Suez. Nearby forces can bring the immediate canal defence to roughly 12 divisions; expect 15–25 within one or two weeks after the threat becomes clear.

## 8. Execution calendar

### Preparation phase: February to late May

1. Build the supply stockpile to at least 25,000, preferably 30,000.
2. Reduce dissent below 1 percent.
3. Redeploy the four motor corps and Northern Army Command to Karachi.
4. Redeploy the principal Caucasus mountain column and Western Army Command to Karachi.
5. Keep the secondary mountain column around Gwadar.
6. Upgrade the light-armour formations; do not delay the entire campaign waiting for perfection.
7. Split the fleet, add commanders and correct screen ratios.
8. Place Transport Groups 1–6 at Karachi and 7–9 at Rangoon.
9. Accumulate at least 15 deployable airbase levels.

### D-Day to D+7

**East:** Load the five-division marine assault in Rangoon. Sail the listed sea route and attack Singapore under the carrier and bombardment groups. Expected arrival is D+4 to D+6. Allow up to three days for combat and unloading.

**West:** Lift the first 18 divisions from Karachi to Basra. A practical first lift is nine motorized and nine mountain divisions. Do not push them out of Basra until supply has arrived.

### D+7 to D+15

**East:** Seize Singapore, unload the nine-division second wave, deploy airbase levels and rebase air cover. Cross to Johor. Move the three-division territorial formation into the bridgehead as mobile reserve.

**West:** Send the motorized column against the exposed British position at An Najaf. When the Soviet/Persian phase is politically active, send the mountain column against Ahvaz. The transports return to Karachi for the headquarters and remaining formations.

### D+15 to D+30

**Malaya:** Advance in two columns. The west column clears Kuala Lumpur, Ipoh and Alor Setar. The east column clears Kuantan and Kota Bahru. Stop at the Siamese boundary; do not turn a quick port seizure into an unplanned Thai campaign.

**Palestine:** Move the motorized column through Karbala and As Zarqa toward Amman and Jerusalem. A lightly contested force should reach the Palestinian approaches around D+25 to D+35.

**Persia:** Advance Ahvaz–Hamadan–Tabriz. A lightly contested column should reach Tabriz around D+30 to D+40.

### D+31 to D+60

**East:** Finish Malaya. Leave six to nine divisions to hold the peninsula and naval base. Begin transferring the five best marines through Colombo toward the Red Sea.

**Suez:** Concentrate 12–18 ground divisions near El Arish and Al Qantarah. Attack Port Said from land while no more than five marines attack Suez amphibiously. Use forward tactical air and naval bombardment. The purpose of the simultaneous attack is to split the canal defence before Commonwealth reinforcements accumulate.

**Baku:** Attack from Tabriz with 12–15 mountain divisions under tactical/close-air support. If Baku contains more than 12 effective divisions, take Tehran and the Caspian coast to create another direction of attack; do not grind head-on.

### D+61 to D+90

**Egypt:** Clear Cairo and Alexandria. Leave six to nine divisions at the canal and key ports. Do not chase British units deep into Africa until the canal supply network is stable.

**Caucasus:** Hold Baku with at least six mountain divisions. Bring the upgraded mobile reserve through and advance Baku–Makhachkala–Khasavyurt–Elista–Astrakhan. Use offensive supply for the decisive 30-day Baku/Astrakhan phase.

**East:** Consolidate Singapore as a convoy, repair and air hub. Do not open an Australia or Indonesia campaign inside the same 90-day window.

Desired D+90 position: Singapore and Malaya secure; Suez/Cairo captured or operationally isolated; Baku held; Astrakhan captured or under deliberate attack. If two of these three theatres are late, halt expansion and restore supply.

## 9. Enemy reaction and decision branches

### Singapore assault fails to progress by D+10

Break off before the marine force is destroyed. Land at Kuantan or Ipoh, establish land-based air cover, isolate Singapore and attack it from Johor. Do not repeat an unsupported frontal landing.

### Britain commits a capital fleet to the Indian Ocean

Avoid a fleet-wide decisive battle. Withdraw transports behind the carrier force, use naval bombers from Colombo/Singapore/Karachi, and fight only under land-based air coverage. The purpose of the navy is to deliver and sustain the armies, not exchange hulls evenly with Britain.

### Basra cannot sustain the landing

If the depot does not reach roughly 1,000 supplies and the mobile column cannot retain fuel, hold Basra and run convoys. Do not drive into damaged Iraqi infrastructure with empty formations.

### Baku has more than 12 defenders

Secure Tehran and the Caspian approach, attack on more than one axis and wait for German pressure. The Indian mountain force is excellent, but a single-axis assault against a reinforced Soviet mountain position wastes manpower.

### Germany begins to collapse

Cancel the Astrakhan exploitation. Hold Baku, Tabriz and the Persian mountain line. These positions preserve oil, shorten the front and force the USSR to divert troops without exposing India in the open steppe.

### Germany does not attack the USSR by August

Complete Singapore and Suez, repair the fleet, upgrade armour and defer the Soviet campaign to spring 1942. India should not open a solo land war against the save's 336 Soviet divisions merely to satisfy the original calendar.

## 10. Loss and logistics controls

- Set a first-90-day manpower loss ceiling of **100 manpower** from the current 423. Stop frontal attacks when this ceiling is approached.
- Never place all 33 western mountain divisions on one poor-infrastructure supply path.
- Keep one transport group empty and available for emergency evacuation or reinforcement.
- Use offensive supply on the Baku spearhead and, if necessary, the canal assault—not universally.
- Do not use model-0 armour as mountain assault infantry. Its role begins after the passes are open.
- Pause after each decisive objective long enough for organization, depot stock and airbase coverage to catch up.

## 11. The commander's daily checks

Every day during the opening fortnight, check:

1. Basra and Singapore depot supply, not merely the national stockpile.
2. Transport and covering-fleet organization.
3. Enemy naval sightings in the Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal and Malacca Strait.
4. Arrival dates after weather or combat changes them.
5. Whether Britain has begun rail movement toward the canal.
6. Whether Soviet formations are leaving Baku, Stalingrad or Astrakhan.

Every seven days, reassess whether the campaign still has one main effort in each theatre. If every formation is moving toward a different prize, the operation has already lost coherence.
