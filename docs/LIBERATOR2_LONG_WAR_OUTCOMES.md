## Resume, do not restart

Deployment target: **AUBM Terrain Prototype P1**. The separate **A Union Before
Midnight V4.2** installation is not updated. Restart the game application after
installation, select the prototype profile, and resume the **1 August 1940
autosave**. No replacement save or new campaign is needed.

Select **LIBERATOR2 - India's Independent Campaigns**, then **Enable the
sovereign-liberator campaign**. Enabling is free, changes no alliance or resource,
and declares no war. If LIBERATOR1 was already enabled in a later save, its flag
is retained: use **LIBERATOR2 - Long-War Objectives and Endings** directly.
That named decision identifies the gameplay pack. The graphical Alpha 27 loading
badge is deliberately unchanged; it is not the add-on version indicator.

Fresh campaigns in this updated profile also load these events, with opt-in
available from 1940. This revision serves sovereign India; it is not a new
all-alliance content overhaul. Joining a major coalition or another route's
commitment suspends active eligibility without erasing earned historical flags.

## Design: an exit, a strategic result, and a lasting legacy

You should not need to conquer an entire major power to get a useful regional
peace. Conversely, a few captured frontier provinces should not dismantle it.
The revision separates those outcomes. Suez and simultaneous Japan/USSR wars
are never compulsory. The player chooses when a war has achieved enough.

| Campaign | Earned political result | Further lasting outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| China | Independent exit at three cities, or Indian puppet offer after a sustained four-city campaign | Optional full independence after two years |
| USSR | Deep offensive permits separate Central Asian republics under Indian puppet control | Paid frontier logistics or Indian industrial development |
| Japan | Existing regional peace plus China/SEA partners remains a complete ending | Optional home-island victory unlocks a larger development choice |
| Tibet | Existing earned Tibetan settlement makes an Indian puppet | 90-day Himalayan consolidation and logistics investment |
| Persia–Afghanistan | Secure Tehran and Kabul under India or its puppets | 90-day western-corridor consolidation and logistics investment |

## China: a stronger result is earned by a stronger campaign

The existing three-city sovereign offer remains: hold **Nanjing (1337), Shanghai
(1338) and Wuhan (1317)** while India fights Japan and its Nanjing puppet U87.
Its cost remains 250 money / 750 supplies, with 60% acceptance and 40% refusal.

The new protection offer adds **Chongqing (1299)** and requires all four cities
held for **60 days**. Nanjing, Wuhan and Chongqing must still legally belong to
U87. Shanghai may remain legally Japanese, as in the reviewed save.

1. The consolidation record starts automatically once the real requirements hold.
2. **Chinese Settlement: Demand an Indian Protectorate** becomes available.
3. Pay **350 money and 1,000 supplies**. Response: **80% accept / 20% refuse**.
   Refusal permits another offer after 90 days. The stronger offer has better
   odds because its leverage is substantially greater, not because puppet terms
   are inherently more attractive than independence.
4. Acceptance first ends U87's Japanese puppet status and withdraws it from
   Tokyo's alliance with the documented white-peace withdrawal setting.
   This affects China's inherited wars, not India's Japanese war.
5. **Ratify the Chinese Protectorate** appears only after China is genuinely
   independent, outside the major alliances, at peace, and back in control of
   Nanjing and Wuhan. Choose **Make China an Indian puppet: +3 dissent**.
   Receive **+4 TC modifier and 2,000 supplies once**, alongside the explicit
   puppet relationship. Alternatively honour full independence; Cancel postpones.

The Chinese state, tag and units survive. This does not resurrect Chiang,
annex China, or transfer Japan-owned Shanghai. No explicit new alliance command
is issued; ordinary engine puppet obligations apply. The transition's engine
behaviour still needs a live acceptance test, especially war membership.

A protection proposal cannot also collect the original sovereign-China reward
in the gap between withdrawal and ratification. A ratified Chinese protectorate
can count as one of the two southern partners for the existing peace dividend.

After an actual puppet relationship has existed for **730 days**, and China is
at peace and India is no longer fighting Japan, an optional independence decision
ends Indian mastery and guarantees China. It does not dissolve a separate
alliance. Cancel retains the puppet with no new scripted cost. Independence
does not repay conquest rewards or automatically negotiate a new equal treaty.

## USSR: the loss point is considerably deeper

**Baku plus Tashkent or Astrakhan remains only the existing frontier milestone.**
It can still support a limited base-rights armistice, but LIBERATOR2 blocks the
old shortcut from that milestone directly to republic releases.

For territorial republic terms, hold all of:

- **Baku (713), Tashkent (1103), Astrakhan (706), Stalingrad (663)**;
- **at least one of Sverdlovsk (1151), Omsk (1138), Moscow (572)**;
- **at least two complete, Soviet-owned republic minimum territories**, with
  every required province controlled by India and those republics not yet existing.

Maintain the conditions for **90 days**. A failed daily check resets the active
attempt, rather than allowing recovery on the last day to rescue it. This is a
scripted control test, not a battle counter or proof of an unbroken supply line.

| Republic | Required minimum provinces |
| --- | --- |
| Turkmenistan / TRK | 1097, 1098 |
| Uzbekistan / UZB | 1099, 1100, 1101, 1102, 1103 |
| Tajikistan / TAJ | 1104, 1105 |
| Kyrgyzstan / KYG | 1106, 1107 |
| Kazakhstan / KAZ | 498, 500, 504, 505, 506, 507 |

Open **Provisional Settlement: Northern Command → Open the Soviet armistice
docket → Demand Indian puppet republics; see terms above**. The existing
Northern campaign/current-victory condition must also be present.

The deep protected offer now receives **80% acceptance / 20% refusal**, with a
90-day retry after refusal. It has no forced base-rights counteroffer. Accepted
terms use the existing transfer commission: snapshot complete republics, transfer
only their legally Soviet-owned occupied territories, then create separate
Indian puppets. Two complete republics do not grant all five. The existing
**+6 dissent** cost of establishing protected republics remains.

The sovereign-republic alternative remains 55/30/15, but requires the same deeper
territorial leverage while this pack is active. Its narrower counteroffer can
now be rejected. Other, non-LIBERATOR2 routes retain their original gates/odds.

After actual protected settlement and peace with the USSR, at least two complete
Indian client republics must exist and control their territory. This verifies the
political result and unlocks **Central Asian Victory: Choose the Lasting
Investment**. Pay **500 money and 2,000 supplies** for exactly one:

- **Frontier network:** +8 TC modifier and +5 supply-output modifier.
- **Indian industry:** +2 base IC each in **Delhi, Bombay and Calcutta** (+6 total).
  All three cities must be Indian-owned and controlled.

The earlier, separate reconstruction decision now accepts protected as well as
sovereign settlements: 300 money / 1,000 supplies buys +1 research, +2 supply
output and -2 dissent once. Previously the protected route was wrongly excluded.

Each actual client republic gets its own optional independence review after
730 days. Release requires that republic at peace and India not fighting the
USSR; India may still be fighting Japan. Previously released clients can still
count for the long-war investment if they remain independent, friendly and secure.

This hardens the **settlement threshold**, not Soviet combat AI, reinforcement
or equipment. It cannot guarantee the USSR fights competently in every world
situation. Reaching the Urals/Omsk through the southern front is a substantial
logistics campaign; attacking while the Soviet army is busy elsewhere remains
much easier than defeating a secure USSR alone.

## Japan: a useful regional ending, or a more ambitious finale

The regional ending still works without invading Japan: complete the Southeast
Asian operational theatre, end the Japanese war, and retain two live partners.
The new Chinese protectorate can count as one. Existing SEA petitions retain
their sovereign-partner terms; this is not a blanket puppet conversion.

For the optional larger legacy, hold **Tokyo (1552), Osaka (1553) and Hiroshima
(1554)** as India for **60 days during the war**. The completed achievement is
retained when peace returns. The postwar investment requires either the existing
**full Japanese armistice** or Japan no longer existing. A limited counteroffer
alone does not qualify. Existing Japanese negotiation odds and territory
transfers have not been increased in this revision.

**After the Home-Island War: The Indian Peace Dividend** costs **500 money and
2,000 supplies**, once, and offers:

- **Blue-water future:** +8 TC, +5 supply output, +2 general research modifier.
- **Industrial future:** +2 base IC each in Delhi, Bombay and Calcutta (+6 total).

These are Indian development programmes, not unimplemented Japanese reparations
or a promise of automatic Japanese annexation. The player chooses one legacy per
major campaign; the Japanese and Soviet programmes can both be earned.

## Independent missions: Tibet and the western corridor

**Tibet:** the reviewed save contains an independent Tibet. Chinese surrender
cannot transfer it. The existing earned path is to finish the Tibetan campaign,
annex it, then choose **Protect Tibet: +4 dissent; earned Tokyo clause waives**
in **The Tibet Settlement Docket**. That restores Tibet as an Indian puppet;
the normal +4 dissent applies unless the earlier earned waiver exists.

Once Tibet actually is an Indian puppet and holds Lhasa, maintain that result
for **90 days**, then pay **200 money / 1,000 supplies** for **+3 TC and +2
supply output once**. No extra Tibet war or major-power victory is required for
the development step. This revision does not add a free diplomatic seizure of
Tibet or change its existing armistice odds.

**Western corridor:** secure **Tehran (shown as Teheran, 1085)** and **Kabul
(2171)** under Indian ownership/control or their actual Indian puppet governments.
India must be at peace with Persia and Afghanistan. Maintain 90 days, then
pay **300 money / 1,000 supplies** for **+5 TC and +3 supply output once**.
This is a bounded capital-security mission, not a claim that every intervening
province or convoy route has been tested for connectivity.

Neither mission requires Japanese, Soviet or Suez achievements. Neither
investment secretly declares war, changes ownership or creates a puppet.

## Counteroffers and the final conference

The Soviet base-rights response and the shared great-power reduced-terms response
now have **Reject ... keep fighting** choices. Rejection clears that negotiation
lock, preserves the war and permits another offer after 90 days. It does not
undo any access the foreign state already offered. Acceptance still invokes the
existing India-scoped peace protocol; no new foreign-scoped peace command is added.

The former **Two Wars, No Empire** title is now **Delhi Conference: The Two-Front
Settlement**, because calling a puppet sphere "no empire" was misleading.
Protected republics and a consenting Chinese protectorate can qualify alongside
independent partners. The existing two-front legacy pays once and requires all
Indian wars ended. Suez remains optional. No charter or mobilisation history resets.

## Verification and limits

Source changes: 40 additional events beyond LIBERATOR1's 51; revisions to the
existing add-on; seven allowlisted old settlement events; one shared great-power
counteroffer. Existing route bridge changes from LIBERATOR1 are retained.

The targeted installer backs up the three replaced event modules and the latest
autosave pair; checks their installed map IDs, republic definitions and source
hashes; refuses to run while Darkest Hour is open; and verifies saves, scenarios,
visual files, launcher settings and the separate normal installation are unchanged.
It never launches or controls the game.

The test suite exercises 56 script-level contracts: time thresholds and resets,
deep versus frontier outcomes, partial territories, stale negotiations, ownership,
legacy-route preservation, one-time costs, protected reconstruction, optional
independence, non-looping cancel exits and counteroffer rejection. Static audits
also check the complete event graph, script syntax, art references and manifest.

Final verification: 56 campaign tests and four menu tests passed; full V4
validation returned zero errors and zero warnings. Wartime, Southeast Asian,
southern-settlement, bespoke-route, global-campaign, Japan, route-consequence,
diplomatic-clarity and strict art audits passed. The installer verified all
protected files unchanged and did not launch the game.

**Not engine-playtested.** Before claiming live verification, test withdrawal
and puppet war membership, persistent-event date restart behaviour, the staged
Soviet transfer/release/peace sequence, and both investment branches. Daily
checks can miss a loss and recapture between checks; they are not continuous
battle telemetry. The political outcomes and payouts are testable, but their
subjective fun and final balance need your playthrough.

All-event language and choice cleanup remains explicitly deferred until this
playthrough is finished. This pack improves its own text and two dangerous
counteroffer screens; it is not a claim that every old event has been rewritten.
