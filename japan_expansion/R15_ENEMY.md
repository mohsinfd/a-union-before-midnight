# R15: the enemy answers Japanese expansion

Three finite enemy preparations replace a passive response to major Japanese gains. Threats count direct Japanese control and actual Japanese puppet controllers derived from the native releasable-country map. Independent allies and neutral governments do not count. This prevents a prompt Ceylon or Malayan puppet release from evading the response. They require a human, independent Japan, the Pacific war, the actual enemy still at war with Japan, and an AI-controlled recipient. They never declare war, transfer territory or give Japan rewards. R14.1 events, existing Allied transport protection and normal AI production remain intact.

| Japanese position | Enemy order, after 30 days to assess the threat | Paid bill | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hawaii in Japanese hands | USA: three Marine divisions, three transport flotillas and four Destroyer flotillas, assembled at San Diego, Los Angeles or San Francisco | 60 manpower, 200 money, 12,000 supplies, 4,000 oil and 2,000 metal | 180 days after payment |
| Rabaul, Port Moresby or Darwin in Japanese hands | Australia: four Infantry divisions and two Interceptor wings, at Brisbane | 58 manpower, 75 money, 5,000 supplies, 1,000 oil and 1,000 metal | 120 days after payment |
| Singapore and Colombo in Japanese hands | Britain: three Infantry divisions, two transports and four Destroyer flotillas, at Aden | 50 manpower, 125 money, 8,000 supplies, 3,000 oil and 1,500 metal | 150 days after payment |

The budget includes supporting personnel in addition to the deployed formations. All new formations use the newest researched model whose service year has arrived. There are no free replacements or unfunded production orders. Marines require at least 1931 research; Infantry at least 1931, Interceptors at least 1930, Destroyers at least 1933, and transports at least 1925. Actual manpower requirements across eligible models are checked against the bill.

The initial Japanese notice means an enemy assessment is under way, not that units have already appeared. The enemy pays once only if the threat still exists, the entire bill is available, and a safe owned-and-controlled assembly site has the required maintained port/airfield. If its money, equipment stocks, manpower, research or facilities are short, the enemy waits and can fund the same one-time order later. It receives no free budget. If the Japanese threat disappears before payment or the diplomatic position changes, the unstarted order closes with a Japanese notice. Normal AI activity continues. A funded force can finish after the original Japanese gain is reversed because its training has already started. It still needs a continuing war, the original recipient, and a safe assembly site. Peace, lost facilities, lost technology or changed controller cancels delivery and returns the exact enemy payment. If the enemy country ceases to exist, a Japanese-side due-date check closes the obsolete order so a recreated country cannot claim it.

Every family is finite and shares no random repeatable grants. Each order has its own names, pending flag, saved payment date, elapsed-time check and private command-execution receipt. Cached choices cannot charge twice or bypass changed conditions. Newly created divisions/flotillas are immediately restored to their actual maximum strength; organisation still recovers normally. The 25-strength Japanese island detachments are untouched.

After the 30-day assessment and again at funding/delivery, a small enemy AI plan gives explicit priority to the relevant objectives. The US plan prioritises Hawaii and Midway, Britain prioritises Colombo and Singapore, and Australia prioritises defending Brisbane/Sydney/Darwin. Plans contain only the native invasion target or garrison province-priority table copied from the appropriate B&I wartime profile, with the relevant entries changed. Existing entries from that native table survive; invasion distances and all unrelated settings are omitted. The native ENG1914_Senussi.ai demonstrates this partial invasion-target format. They do not overwrite military construction ratios or the rest of the active AI. Later native AI switches remain enabled. These are priorities for the existing engine, not a promise that it will keep every fleet grouped or coordinate an invasion perfectly.

No game is run during the source/transaction audit. Real fleet use, invasion timing and difficulty remain playtest observations.

Offline suite: `python r15_enemy_review.py`, 15 tests covering all three families, actual budgets, waiting without free grants, contemporary researched equipment, safe facilities/ownership, exact peace refunds, disappeared governments, cached selections, same-group transport screens, native AI partials and idempotence.
