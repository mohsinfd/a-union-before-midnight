# Japan Rising R15

R15 adds reactions to Japanese victories, choices after serious losses, and competing military preparations. It builds on R14.1. Start a new 1933 campaign through the existing Japan Rising JX1 Alpha2 shortcut; confirm R15 in the scenario and welcome event. The game is never started by the installer or audits.

## What changes during a campaign

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| From 1937, war with China, Shanghai or Nanjing held and China still has 61+ formations | Choose three additional land divisions or prepare escorts, carrier aircraft and convoy protection for the next war. |
| Singapore captured during the Pacific war | Defend Truk, Saipan and Kwajalein with full Garrisons, AA attachments and escorts, or assemble Marines, Mountain troops and troop transports at Japanese-owned Singapore. |
| An American mainland foothold survives 30 days | Commit 104 manpower to six Infantry and two Mountain divisions, or choose quicker home-base and shipping consolidation. The field army assembles in Japan; it must be transported across the Pacific. |
| Three fleet carriers lost and fewer than four remain | Pay for two Light Carriers, two Light CAGs and four Destroyers over 330 days, or form two Naval Bomber wings and a fighter wing over 180 days. |
| A previously held major anchorage is captured by an enemy | Rebuild convoy capacity quickly or prepare Marines and troop shipping for a counterattack. Truk, Rabaul, Singapore and Pearl Harbor are recognised; actual hostile countries are checked. Recapture does not erase the historical setback. |

All these formations use researched, date-appropriate equipment. Their full crews are paid from the real manpower pool. Any reserved crew surplus returns when the actual model is selected. Newly created map formations receive individually targeted strength restoration up to their actual allowed maximum. Organisation, supply and mobilisation rules still apply. CAGs and AA brigades enter the deployment pool for manual fitting.

The original quarter-strength local detachments remain unchanged. The new full Garrisons are separate formations. No permanent production discounts, new unit classes, compulsory wars or alliance changes are added by R15.

## A commitment costs time as well as stocks

The training and transport staff can support one R15 major preparation at a time. Regular production and existing R14.1 programmes remain available. Funding one new programme makes the other new funded choices wait. Completing a stage closes its alternative.

You can close a decision window without changing anything. A private funding date keeps the original delivery timetable intact, even if you reopen the pending decision repeatedly.

If delivery conditions fail, the full original payment returns. Strategic preparations can be reconsidered after 90 days if their situation qualifies again. Recovery programmes are one funded attempt; an automatic refund closes that recovery chapter.

If a more urgent crisis develops, reopen the current programme and cancel it permanently. All paid manpower, oil and metal return, along with 75% of supplies. Money and the remaining 25% of supplies are spent. Cancellation frees the staff for another programme. It grants no formation or completion reward, and a queued old delivery cannot release another programme's staff or award its units.

## The opposing countries react

| Japanese success | Enemy preparation after assessment and payment |
|---|---|
| Hawaii held by Japan or a qualifying Japanese client | America prepares three Marine divisions, four Destroyers and three troop transports at a safe West Coast base; 180 days after funding. |
| Japanese forces threaten Australia through Rabaul, Port Moresby or Darwin | Australia prepares four Infantry divisions and two Interceptor wings at Brisbane; 120 days after funding. |
| Singapore and Colombo held by Japan or qualifying clients | Britain prepares three Infantry divisions, four Destroyers and two troop transports at Aden; 150 days after funding. |

There is a 30-day assessment first. Target priorities are adjusted then; a poor enemy can wait for the required budget before raising the finite force. Money, manpower, supplies, oil and metal are actually paid. If safe bases or the war no longer permit delivery, a surviving government receives its investment back. New units never appear in Japanese-controlled or neutral bases. Transport flotillas initially form in the same fleet as their screens.

The AI plans change only the relevant invasion targets or base-defence priorities. They preserve other active settings and R14.1's transport-construction protections. The engine may regroup fleets or replace these priorities through its native AI switches. These are funded forces and orders, not guaranteed coordinated invasions.

## What is preserved

All 2,941 R14.1 event IDs, their compiled event logic and existing English are checked against the prior installed build. The welcome/scenario version labels change. R14.1's sixty-formation decisive Chinese surrender, safe settlements, peace choices, manpower/resource systems, unique artwork and specialist unlocks remain.

The R15 packet comparison uses one coherent production-programme choice per packet, current research and a generous minister/slider/gearing stress scenario. It helped identify and correct an overly attractive shore-air option. See R15_COST_AUDIT.md; arithmetic is not a production-screen measurement or proof of equal combat utility.

Detailed gates, budgets and cascades: R15_STRATEGY.md, R15_RECOVERY.md and R15_ENEMY.md. Fresh evidence: R15_COLD_AUDIT.json, R15_INSTALL_RECEIPT.json and FINAL_INSTALL_REVIEW.json.

No game or launcher is run by this work. Startup, actual UI layout, enemy execution, automatic oil/convoy routing and long-campaign Hard balance remain player tests.
