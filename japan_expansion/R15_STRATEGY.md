# R15: competing Japanese commitments

These are three situational investments. They allocate the same national reserve of money, supplies, oil and trained people. Only one R15 strategic or recovery investment can be under way at a time. Each completed stage permanently closes its other alternative; it does not select a diplomatic route, start a war, or close unrelated R14.1 content. Leaving an offer available is free. Permanent dismissal is explicit.

## 1. Finish China or prepare the fleet — from 1937

Available during the Nationalist Chinese war, before the Pacific opening, after Japan controls Shanghai or Nanjing and China still fields at least 61 formations.

| Commitment | Payment | Delay | Concrete result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reinforce the Chinese offensive | 300 money, 7,000 supplies, 39 manpower, 500 oil | 180 days | Two Infantry and one Mountain division at Tokyo, using current researched equipment. May finish after peace in China. |
| Prepare the carrier and escort reserve | 350 money, 7,000 supplies, up to 15.5 manpower, 1,800 oil | 240 days | Two Destroyer flotillas at Tokyo, two CAG attachments in the deployment pool and 60 convoy escorts. Twelve training staff return; the full model manpower remains assigned to the equipment. May finish after peace in China or the Pacific opening. |

The ground choice delivers sooner and needs much more permanent manpower. The naval choice protects scarce recruits and prepares the next war, but leaves the present offensive without new divisions. CAGs need existing or future carriers; this grants no carrier hulls or aircraft experience.

## 2. Hold the Pacific or sail west — from 1941

Available after the Pacific opening while fighting America or Britain, with Singapore under Japanese control.

| Commitment | Payment | Delay | Concrete result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defend the Pacific bases | 350 money, 7,500 supplies, 39 manpower, 1,000 oil | 210 days | One full Garrison each at Truk, Saipan and Kwajalein; three researched AA brigades in the deployment pool for manual assignment; 100 convoy escorts. The three Japanese-owned ports must remain usable. |
| Form the Indian Ocean expedition | 450 money, 9,000 supplies, up to 29 manpower, 5,000 oil | 240 days | One Marine and one Mountain division plus two troop-transport flotillas at Japanese-owned Singapore; 2,000 oil remains for operations. Requires normal Marine and transport research and a maintained port. |

The Pacific choice gives fixed defence at three actual bases and shipping protection. The western choice creates a mobile offensive force but buys no additional defence for those islands. Singapore must be owned as well as controlled for deployment; the existing Malayan settlement can retain the base. These are map transport flotillas, not convoy transport points.

## 3. A new American field army or home consolidation — from 1942

Available after Japan's American landing has been recorded for at least 30 days, while Japan is still at war with America and currently controls San Diego, Los Angeles, San Francisco or Seattle. The new army also requires that foothold at delivery. All new formations assemble safely in Japan, not in contested American land.

| Commitment | Payment | Delay | Concrete result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raise the next army for America | 800 money, 22,000 supplies, 104 manpower, 6,000 oil | 270 days | Six Infantry and two Mountain divisions at Tokyo, with current researched equipment. Japan must transport the force across the Pacific. |
| Strengthen home bases and shipping | 450 money, 11,000 supplies, 40 manpower, 2,500 oil | 150 days | One full Garrison each at Tokyo, Yokohama, Hiroshima and Nagoya; 180 convoy transports, 100 convoy escorts and 4,000 supplies. |

The field army requires much more manpower and almost twice as long. Consolidation releases the shared preparation capacity sooner and protects Japanese factories and shipping, but creates no additional field army. The smaller existing American-front supply/recruitment programmes remain available independently.

## Delivery and cancellation

Every equipment branch selects the newest researched model whose service year has arrived; future technology cannot produce future equipment early. Ordinary native formations and attachments are used. Manpower reserves cover the full possible model bill; any unused balance is returned at delivery. New formations are restored only to their actual allowed maximum strength. Organisation and later losses remain normal.

Every branch requires its real research and safe deployment sites both when commissioned and at delivery. The three AA brigades require normal AA research and use eligible formations' ordinary attachment slots; they are not static provincial AA construction. For a branch requiring continued war, peace cancels delivery. A lost site or otherwise invalid delivery also returns every paid input once and releases only that project's shared commitment. No partial reward is accepted. Refunds reopen the uncompleted stage after the common waiting period; successful delivery closes it permanently. Deferred windows and repeated clicks cannot charge or deliver twice.

A player can abandon a pending commitment from the same decision card. This returns the reserved manpower, oil and metal, plus 75% of the supplies. Money and the remaining supplies are spent; that stage closes permanently. This provides a costly escape when a carrier disaster or another urgent need makes the original commitment untenable. It cannot release another project's lock or permit the old delivery to run later.

No game is launched by the module or its tests. These scripts do not edit alliances, wars, sovereignty, cores, existing units or R14.1 construction modifiers.
